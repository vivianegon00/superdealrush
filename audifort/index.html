01

Why it gets louder at night

Almost everyone with persistent tinnitus reports the same thing: it's worst in bed. That isn't your imagination, and it isn't the ringing getting stronger.

During the day there's a floor of background sound — traffic, appliances, conversation — and your brain has plenty of competing input. At night that floor drops away, and the internal signal becomes the loudest thing in the room. Add the fact that you're lying still with nothing to occupy your attention, and it takes over.

The volume didn't change. The competition did.

This matters practically, because it points at the single most useful thing most people haven't tried properly.

02

Building a routine around it

Nothing single-handedly fixes tinnitus. What tends to move the needle is having several things working at once — and these are the pieces worth putting in place whatever else you decide to try.

Sound enrichment

Giving the ringing something to compete with — a fan, a white-noise machine, rain audio, the radio low — is well supported and easy to start tonight. The goal isn't to drown it out. It's to stop it being the only thing in the room, which is what lets your attention drift off it.

Protecting the hearing you still have

Loud noise exposure makes tinnitus worse over time, and that damage doesn't come back. Ear protection at concerts, with power tools, on a mower — dull advice, but it's the one that changes where you are in five years.

Sleep and stress

Tinnitus and poor sleep feed each other in both directions: the ringing keeps you awake, and being exhausted makes you less able to tune it out. Breaking that loop tends to reduce how intrusive the ringing feels even though nothing about the sound itself changed.

Attention-based therapy

Structured programmes built on cognitive behavioural therapy have solid clinical support. They don't remove the sound — they change how much of your attention it captures. Worth asking an audiologist about if the ringing is affecting your sleep or concentration.

How to try one properly

Wanting to try something is reasonable. The trick is setting it up so you actually learn something from the attempt instead of losing track of whether it helped.

Illustrative image supplied by the manufacturer.

Set the window before you start. Decide now that you'll judge it at eight weeks. A deadline is what turns it into a real test instead of an open-ended habit.

Read the label yourself. Not the sales page — the actual Supplement Facts panel. If a blend is listed without per-ingredient amounts, you can't compare it to anything, and you should know that going in.

Buy where the guarantee is real. Nutrition takes weeks to show anything. A refund window shorter than your trial period isn't a guarantee, it's a formality — check the number of days before you order, not after.

Change one thing at a time. If you start a supplement, a white-noise machine and a new sleep routine in the same week, you'll never know which one did anything.

04

When to see a doctor instead

Don't start with a supplement if any of this applies

Ringing that started suddenly, affects only one ear, pulses in time with your heartbeat, or arrives alongside hearing loss, dizziness or ear pain.

Each of those points at causes that can be identified and sometimes treated — and for some of them, how quickly you're seen genuinely changes the outcome. A supplement is not the right first move here.

05

Questions people actually ask

Can a supplement make tinnitus go away?

No. Nothing sold as a supplement has been shown to eliminate tinnitus. The realistic goal for anything in this category is feeling less bothered by it, and even that isn't guaranteed.

How long before I'd know if something is working?

Give it eight weeks. Anything promising results in days is describing marketing, not physiology.

Is it safe with my medication?

Ask your doctor or pharmacist, and bring the actual label with you. Herbal ingredients can interact with blood thinners, blood pressure medication and diabetes medication. This isn't a question to settle from a website.

Should I change anything else at the same time?

Get sound into the room at night whatever else you try — a fan or a rain track works. It costs nothing, and it actually makes the rest easier to judge, because you're less fixated on the ringing while you wait.
